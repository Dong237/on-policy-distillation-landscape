# WP0 Survey Replication and Citation Audit

## Research Memo

Organizing source audited: urlA Survey of On-Policy Distillation for Large Language Modelsturn0search3. My bottom line is that the survey’s high-level story is directionally right, but its field boundary is too loose. The strict core of on-policy distillation is narrower than the survey implies: the durable spine of the field is methods that train on **student-generated rollouts** while applying **teacher-like supervision on those exact rollouts** and **feeding that supervision into the objective**. That core is well supported by GKD, MiniLLM, later white-box OPD variants, and the strongest teacher-free self-distillation papers. citeturn11view1turn14view0turn33search0turn33search1turn33search2turn33search3turn26view1turn31view1

The survey’s core thesis is also basically correct. OPD exists because standard offline KD and teacher-trace SFT suffer a train–test mismatch: the student is optimized on teacher or dataset trajectories, then evaluated on its **own** trajectories at inference time. GKD states this directly as a distribution-mismatch problem and motivates training on self-generated outputs with teacher probabilities on those trajectories; MiniLLM makes an aligned sequence-level argument and recasts the objective as reverse-KL / policy-gradient learning over student samples rather than teacher samples. citeturn11view1turn14view0

Where I disagree with the survey is scope control. Some cited methods are only **partly** on-policy, or are adjacent instead of strict. DistiLLM explicitly advertises an **adaptive off-policy** recipe; DAIL explicitly calls itself an **offline learning method** even though it uses mixed-policy generation when constructing synthetic traces; SPIN relies on previous-iteration self-generated negatives against human positives rather than teacher supervision on current-policy rollouts; and ORPO-Distill appears to be mixed-policy preference optimization rather than clean trajectory-mounted distillation. Those methods are worth cataloging, but they should not anchor the strict OPD landscape. citeturn6view2turn20view0turn29view0turn32search0turn32search7

The survey’s three-axis taxonomy is useful but incomplete. “Feedback signal,” “teacher access,” and “loss granularity” are real structure in the literature, and the survey table captures that well. But for a repository intended to be used as a field map, a **fourth axis is mandatory**: **rollout provenance and supervision consumption**. Without that fourth gate, the taxonomy collapses teacher-free self-distillation that is genuinely strict OPD, such as OPSD and SDPO, into the same bucket as adjacent self-play or preference methods such as SPIN. citeturn17view0turn26view1turn31view1turn29view0

On industrial claims, public evidence is strongest for entity["product","Qwen3","language model family"] and entity["product","Nemotron-Cascade 2","Mixture-of-Experts language model"], where the official reports explicitly mention on-policy distillation. Evidence is weaker for entity["product","Gemma 2","open language model family"], whose report clearly documents large-scale knowledge distillation but not strict on-policy machinery, and only partial for entity["product","MiMo-V2-Flash","Mixture-of-Experts reasoning model"], whose public report mentions an MOPD post-training paradigm but does not, in the accessible snippets, fully substantiate the survey’s more specific mechanism claims. citeturn38view0turn38view1turn37view2turn38view5turn37view1turn39search1turn39search4

My recommendation for the repository is simple: treat the survey as a good literature index, not a canonical ontology. The core strict-OPD arc appears to run from GKD and MiniLLM to white-box stabilizing variants such as G-OPD, Entropy-Aware OPD, Veto, and Revisiting OPD, then to teacher-free/privileged-context methods such as OPSD and SDPO. Everything else should be explicitly labeled as borderline, partial, adjacent, or unclear rather than silently absorbed into “OPD.” citeturn11view1turn14view0turn33search0turn33search1turn33search2turn33search3turn26view1turn31view1

## Taxonomy Reconstruction

The survey reconstructs OPD along three main axes: feedback signal, teacher access, and loss granularity. That framing mostly survives audit, but it is not enough for repository-quality classification. The decisive discriminator is whether **current student rollouts** receive **trajectory-mounted supervision** that is **actually consumed by the training objective**. Comparing the survey table with GKD, MiniLLM, OVD, OPSD, SDPO, SPIN, and DAIL makes that gap clear. citeturn17view0turn11view1turn14view0turn24view1turn26view1turn31view1turn29view0turn20view0

| dimension | survey split | audit reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| feedback signal | logit-based; outcome/reward-guided; self-play/teacher-free | Keep, but split “reward-guided” into **distillation-relevant reward/score supervision** vs **pure RL reward**. Pure RLVR/GRPO/RLHF is not strict OPD unless teacher-like supervision is mounted on student rollouts and enters the loss. |
| teacher access | white-box; black-box/API; teacher-free; multi-teacher | Keep, but split “teacher-free” into **privileged-context self-teachers** (strict or near-strict, e.g., OPSD/SDPO) and **self-play preference/self-training** (often adjacent, e.g., SPIN). Human expert solutions with privileged prompting should also be tracked separately. |
| loss granularity | token-level; sequence-level; hybrid/adaptive | Keep. This is one of the most stable parts of the survey. Token-level and sequence-level OPD are genuinely different optimization regimes, not just implementation detail. |
| rollout provenance | not explicit | Add a mandatory axis: **current student**, **teacher-mixed**, **previous-iteration**, **offline synthetic**, **mixed-policy**, or **teacher demos replaced after scoring**. This axis separates strict OPD from merely related methods. |
| strictness gate | not explicit | Add a repository gate: **strict_opd** iff current student rollouts exist, supervision is applied on those rollouts, and the loss consumes that supervision. Otherwise use borderline_strict, partial_opd, adjacent, not_opd, or unclear. |

A compact field map that fits the evidence is this: **white-box logit OPD** (GKD, MiniLLM, G-OPD, Entropy-Aware, Veto, Revisiting), **black-box / score-based OPD** (OVD, and possibly some mixed preference variants), **privileged-context self-distillation** (OPSD, SDPO), and **adjacent families that should not be auto-promoted to strict OPD** (DistiLLM, DAIL, SPIN, ORPO-Distill, offline KD, static preference optimization, reward-only RL). citeturn11view1turn14view0turn33search0turn33search1turn33search2turn33search3turn24view1turn26view1turn31view1turn6view2turn20view0turn29view0turn32search0

## Gap Map

The first-pass audit suggests that the survey’s biggest problem is **boundary discipline**, not a catastrophic failure to list papers. I did not find an obvious publicly visible **core strict-OPD** family from the survey’s 2024–early-2026 window that was plainly absent from the survey table. The more urgent next work is a second-pass audit of recent methods whose titles imply OPD but whose strictness depends on implementation details. citeturn17view0
