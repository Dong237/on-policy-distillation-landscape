# From Theory to Production: An Implementation-Aware Map of On-Policy Distillation for Text-Only LLMs

## Methodological Taxonomy of On-Policy Distillation

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has emerged as a pivotal technique in the post-training landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs), offering a mechanism to refine model capabilities through dense, token-level supervision derived from student-generated outputs [[12](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25562v1), [69](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00626)]. The core principle involves a student policy generating its own trajectories or sequences during the training process, which are then used to compute a loss function with guidance from a teacher, verifier, reward model, or reference model [[9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12125), [42](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12125v1)]. This approach directly addresses the limitations of offline knowledge distillation, where students learn from a static dataset generated by a teacher, potentially leaving them untested on novel behaviors exhibited by an evolving student policy [[80](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25110)]. By evaluating teacher feedback on student-generated rollouts, OPD aims to create a more dynamic and adaptive learning loop [[12](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25562v1)]. The provided body of work reveals a rich and rapidly evolving taxonomy of OPD methods, which can be structured into a hybrid framework integrating both core methodological families and their primary application domains. This structure allows for a nuanced understanding of how different variants address specific challenges in LLM development, from enhancing reasoning to improving efficiency. The foundational variant, often termed Standard OPD, establishes this paradigm by using a distinct external teacher model to provide logit-level supervision over the student's self-sampled completions [[8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15260), [70](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18734)]. This method is frequently formalized using a reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the student's and teacher's predicted token distributions at each step along the trajectory, effectively guiding the student to mimic the teacher's probabilistic choices [[23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10688), [75](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22495v2)].

A significant evolution from standard OPD is the development of Black-Box On-Policy Distillation. This category addresses scenarios where direct access to the teacher model's internal logits is unavailable, a common constraint in proprietary or third-party systems [[48](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10643)]. Instead of relying on explicit probability distributions, black-box methods must infer the teacher's desired output distribution indirectly. One prominent approach to this challenge is the Generative Adversarial Distillation (GAD) framework, which adapts the principles of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to the distillation problem [[51](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10643v1)]. In GAD, the student model acts as the generator, producing on-policy rollouts, while a discriminator learns to distinguish between these student-generated outputs and authentic samples from the black-box teacher [[51](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10643v1), [52](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/weifuru_black-box-on-policy-distillation-of-large-activity-7394925141274763264-bFKj)]. Through this adversarial training process, the student is implicitly encouraged to produce outputs that are statistically indistinguishable from those of the teacher, thus achieving distillation without needing privileged access to its internal representations or logits [[51](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10643v1)]. While this area is less mature than its white-box counterpart, it represents a critical direction for making OPD applicable in real-world industrial settings where models may not be fully open-source [[48](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10643)]. The distinction between white-box and black-box access remains a fundamental axis in classifying OPD methods, with the former being more established due to its reliance on well-understood KL-divergence-based losses [[9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12125), [170](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1472.pdf)].

Further expanding the methodological landscape are hybrid paradigms that integrate OPD with other training techniques, most notably Reinforcement Learning (RL). These OPD+RL or OPD+RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifier/Feedback Reward) hybrids leverage the complementary strengths of both approaches. RL excels at optimizing for long-horizon goals and sparse rewards, such as solving a complex mathematical problem correctly, whereas OPD provides dense, corrective feedback at every single token generation step [[75](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22495v2), [163](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/t/2026-02-10/doc-inhmihnk1356354.shtml?froms=ggmp)]. This synergy has led to the proposal of novel post-training pipelines where OPD serves as a crucial intermediate stage. For instance, one proposed workflow involves an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase, followed by a round of RLVR to align the model with high-level objectives, and culminating in an OPSD stage to compact and refine the learned reasoning process [[151](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06188v1)]. This final step is motivated by the observation that RLVR can teach verbose reasoning traces, and subsequent self-distillation can compress these traces without significant performance degradation, leading to more efficient models [[101](https://arxiv.org/list/cs/new)]. Other research demonstrates that even within an RL framework, on-policy algorithms like on-policy DPO can outperform their off-policy counterparts, suggesting that the on-policy nature of the rollouts themselves confers benefits in terms of stability and convergence speed [[84](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08421), [130](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2601.08421)]. These hybrid models underscore a trend towards more sophisticated and multi-stage training regimes that strategically combine the strengths of different learning paradigms to achieve superior results.

## The Rise of Self-Distillation and Hybrid Paradigms

While standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) provides a powerful mechanism for transferring knowledge from a dedicated teacher model to a student, a significant trend in recent research has been the rise of On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) [[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10674v1), [159](https://swift.readthedocs.io/zh-cn/latest/Instruction/GKD.html)]. This paradigm represents a crucial evolution, simplifying the training infrastructure by eliminating the need for an external teacher model altogether [[162](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2024928733522305798)]. In OPSD, the same model simultaneously acts as both the student and the teacher, leveraging a clever mechanism to provide privileged information [[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10674v1), [162](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2024928733522305798)]. During training, the model generates a sequence (a rollout) and then uses its own parameters—often from a slightly earlier training iteration or a separate, slowly updated copy—as the "privileged" teacher to supervise its own generation [[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10674v1), [160](https://chatpaper.com/zh-CN/chatpaper/paper/276256)]. This self-supervision loop enables a form of continual learning, where the model can iteratively improve by learning from its own mistakes and refining its internal reasoning processes [[114](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2004574638794089414), [158](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2004506304065065334)]. The effectiveness of this approach has been demonstrated across a range of tasks, particularly in mathematical reasoning, where OPSD has been shown to match the performance of more complex reinforcement learning with value regularization (RLVR) methods like GRPO with significantly improved token efficiency [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18734v3), [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18734)]. The simplicity and efficacy of OPSD have made it a popular choice for researchers seeking to implement on-policy training without the overhead of managing and serving a separate teacher model [[164](https://post.smzdm.com/p/aogzqp4r)].

Beyond simple self-distillation, the concept has been extended into more sophisticated hybrid paradigms that combine the strengths of multiple models or learning signals. One such advanced variant is Multi-Teacher or Multi-Agent On-Policy Distillation (MOPD). This approach moves beyond a single teacher-student dyad and instead employs multiple specialized teacher models to guide a single, unified student [[137](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2031622077107659474)]. For example, a system might have one teacher expert in mathematics, another in coding, and a third in general instruction following. The student model would then be trained on rollouts guided by these diverse teachers, allowing it to absorb a broader range of skills [[29](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19220v2)]. DeepSeek's V4 architecture provides a concrete and compelling example of this industrial-scale application, where MOPD is used as a core post-training primitive to merge several domain-specific specialist models into a single, highly capable generalist model [[137](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2031622077107659474), [150](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yumo-xu_deepseekv4-opd-mopd-activity-7455325567341727745-w-ey)]. This method acknowledges that different facets of intelligence may require different expertise and offers a principled way to consolidate them. Similarly, multi-agent debate systems represent a dynamic form of MOPD, where a structured collaboration between multiple LLM agents decomposes complex tasks, debates potential solutions, and verifies the final outcome, creating a rich and challenging dataset for distillation [[59](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03743v1), [62](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03743)]. These hybrid paradigms demonstrate a maturation of the OPD concept, moving from simple teacher-student relationships toward more complex, collaborative knowledge transfer ecosystems.

Another significant extension of the self-distillation concept is Iterative Self-Distillation, which focuses on progressively refining a model's capabilities through repeated cycles of self-teaching. Methods like SIKeD (Self-guided Iterative Knowledge Distillation) exemplify this approach, demonstrating significant performance gains over traditional distillation techniques, especially when applied to smaller models [[111](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.513/), [112](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ozTREVBARB)]. This iterative process can be seen as a form of self-evolution, where the model gradually improves its own reasoning and problem-solving abilities over successive training stages [[45](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.686.pdf)]. CRISP (Compressed Reasoning via Iterative Self-Policy Distillation) takes this a step further by combining iterative self-distillation with the goal of reasoning compression [[41](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05433), [149](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05433v6)]. It requires no ground-truth answers, no external reward engineering, and no difficulty estimation, making it a remarkably autonomous training procedure [[149](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05433v6)]. By repeatedly having the model generate solutions and then distill from itself on those solutions, these methods can dramatically reduce the length of reasoning traces while preserving or even improving accuracy, as seen in the case of OPSDC (On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Compression), which achieved up to a 57% reduction in tokens on the MATH-500 benchmark [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05433v4), [144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05433?ref=taaft&utm_source=taaft&utm_medium=referral), [146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05433v2?ref=taaft&utm_source=taaft&utm_medium=referral)]. This ability to autonomously compress and improve reasoning makes iterative self-distillation a powerful tool for creating more efficient and compact yet highly capable reasoning models. The underlying theme across these advancements is a move towards greater autonomy and efficiency, reducing reliance on external data sources and human annotation while enabling models to engage in a continuous cycle of self-improvement.

## Industrial Post-Training Pipelines Leveraging On-Policy Distillation

The transition of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) from academic research to practical industrial application is perhaps best exemplified by the detailed technical reports and blog posts from Chinese AI labs, particularly DeepSeek. Their public documentation provides a rare and valuable glimpse into the inner workings of modern LLM post-training pipelines, revealing OPD—not as a peripheral technique but as a central, indispensable component for achieving state-of-the-art performance [[137](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2031622077107659474), [138](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2033786457958135733)]. DeepSeek's approach, especially evident in the progression from DeepSeek-V3 to DeepSeek-V4, showcases a sophisticated, multi-stage process heavily reliant on various forms of on-policy distillation [[122](https://hyper.ai/cn/papers/DeepSeek-V4), [123](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02556v1)]. The pipeline begins with the creation of specialist models, where individual models are trained on specific domains such as mathematics, coding, or general conversational skills [[29](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19220v2), [138](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2033786457958135733)]. Once these experts are developed, the core of the pipeline involves a multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) stage. Here, the knowledge from the various specialist models is distilled into a single, unified student model, effectively fusing their capabilities into a generalist agent [[137](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2031622077107659474), [150](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yumo-xu_deepseekv4-opd-mopd-activity-7455325567341727745-w-ey)]. This strategy acknowledges that different competencies benefit from specialized training and provides a principled method for consolidation. The adoption of MOPD as a new post-training primitive in their 2026 reports underscores its perceived importance and success in creating competitive models [[150](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yumo-xu_deepseekv4-opd-mopd-activity-7455325567341727745-w-ey)].

DeepSeek's public materials also shed light on the scale and complexity of these operations. For instance, the distillation process can involve large teacher models being used to train smaller student models, a common practice for creating efficient, deployable versions of powerful base models [[88](https://app.dahecube.com/nweb/news/20250205/225494n8bc64153d2a.htm?artid=225494)]. The DeepSeek-R1 model, for example, was created by distilling knowledge from larger teachers [[87](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleksagordic_deepseek-v4-pro-summary-building-llms-is-activity-7454215953606946817-U6wK)]. This indicates that OPD is not only used for capability enhancement but also for model compression and optimization. Furthermore, the company's technical reports mention the use of extensive, high-quality datasets for post-training, including over 32 trillion tokens for DeepSeek-V4, followed by a comprehensive post-training pipeline involving distillation and mixed reinforcement learning [[122](https://hyper.ai/cn/papers/DeepSeek-V4), [123](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02556v1)]. This highlights that OPD does not exist in isolation but is part of a larger ecosystem of training techniques, including reinforcement learning, that are combined to fine-tune model behavior [[123](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02556v1)]. The emphasis on a "comprehensive post-training pipeline" suggests a holistic approach to model optimization, where OPD plays a crucial role in the final stages of alignment and capability refinement [[138](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2033786457958135733)]. The detailed documentation of this process, available through blogs and technical reports, provides a strong case study for how OPD is implemented at scale in a production environment.

The influence of these industrial practices extends beyond DeepSeek. The GLM-5 model from Zhipu AI is also noted to have incorporated OPD into its post-training pipeline, alongside other architectural innovations like a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design [[139](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404282437_Marco-MoE_Open_Multilingual_Mixture-of-Expert_Language_Models_with_Efficient_Upcycling)]. This corroborates that the use of OPD is not an isolated strategy but is becoming a recognized and adopted technique among leading developers of open-source LLMs [[56](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0740624X26000304)]. The fact that multiple independent projects are converging on similar methodologies suggests a shared understanding of the technique's value. The industrial adoption of OPD appears to be driven by its proven effectiveness in improving model performance, particularly in complex reasoning tasks, and its utility in creating more efficient models through techniques like reasoning compression [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05433v4), [147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05433)]. Companies are investing in the infrastructure required to run these intensive training loops, including the computational resources for asynchronous generation to accelerate training and the data management systems to handle massive, curated datasets [[39](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05940v1), [122](https://hyper.ai/cn/papers/DeepSeek-V4)]. The availability of some implementations and scaling laws as open-source resources further indicates a trend towards transparency and reproducibility within the industry, although many details remain proprietary [[103](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24762v1)]. The collective evidence points to OPD as a foundational technology in the modern LLM development toolkit, essential for moving models from competent base architectures to highly specialized and efficient end products.

## Application Domains: Reasoning, Instruction Tuning, and Agentic Systems

The most mature and impactful application domain for On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of text-only Large Language Models. A vast majority of the research in this area targets mathematical reasoning, code generation, and long-chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, as these tasks are characterized by complex search spaces and sparse reward signals, making them ideal candidates for the dense, step-by-step supervision that OPD provides [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18734v3), [28](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09883v1), [163](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/t/2026-02-10/doc-inhmihnk1356354.shtml?froms=ggmp)]. Studies consistently demonstrate the efficacy of OPD on standard benchmarks such as GSM8K, MATH, AIME, and LiveCodeBench, where models trained with OPD or its variants show significant performance improvements over baseline models and even outperform more complex reinforcement learning methods [[28](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09883v1), [79](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=31wkVZXEaJ), [133](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.923.pdf)]. For instance, OPSD has been shown to match the performance of GRPO (a type of RLVR) with better token efficiency, while other methods like OVD (On-policy Verbal Distillation) dramatically reduce memory consumption during this on-policy training process [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18734v3), [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21968v1)]. The core advantage in these domains is that OPD allows the model to learn from its self-generated mistakes in a structured way, correcting erroneous reasoning paths at each step rather than just receiving a binary pass/fail verdict at the end of a long chain [[43](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.822.pdf), [54](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3zKtaqxLhW)].

Instruction tuning is another key area where OPD principles are proving valuable. While traditional instruction tuning relies on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with a fixed dataset, OPD offers a dynamic alternative. By having the model generate its own responses to instructions and then distilling from a teacher (which could be a stronger model, a verifier, or even itself), the training process can become more interactive and adaptive [[53](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yu-cao-876b99277_self-distilled-reasoner-on-policy-self-distillation-activity-7420563486763753473-W4Ij)]. This approach can help the model learn to follow complex, multi-turn instructions more effectively, especially when these instructions involve rigid constraints or detailed schemas that are difficult to capture in a static dataset [[53](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yu-cao-876b99277_self-distilled-reasoner-on-policy-self-distillation-activity-7420563486763753473-W4Ij)]. The work by Agarwal et al. (2024) specifically validates their method on the FLAN instruction tuning dataset, showing that on-policy training can be effective for this purpose [[54](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3zKtaqxLhW)]. Furthermore, the integration of OPD into multi-stage pipelines, such as the proposed SFT → RLVR → OPSD workflow, suggests a sophisticated approach to instruction following where initial alignment is refined and compressed through on-policy techniques [[151](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06188v1)]. This allows for a separation of concerns: SFT can instill broad capabilities, RLVR can align with complex preferences, and OPD can polish the execution of these capabilities with greater efficiency and fidelity [[10](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.376.pdf), [151](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06188v1)]. This layered approach mirrors the curriculum-based learning strategies seen in human education, where foundational knowledge is built before moving on to more complex, specialized training [[10](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.376.pdf)].

An emerging and highly promising frontier for OPD is its application to training agentic and tool-using LLMs. Agentic systems require models to perform complex, multi-step tasks by decomposing problems, selecting and executing tools, and verifying results—all in a long-horizon setting [[46](https://hal.science/hal-05525481v1/file/_Sok_Agent_Skills.pdf)]. While existing benchmarks often fail to capture the messier environments of production deployments, the principles of OPD are naturally suited to this challenge [[46](https://hal.science/hal-05525481v1/file/_Sok_Agent_Skills.pdf)]. Multi-agent distillation systems, for example, leverage structured collaboration between multiple LLM agents to decompose, debate, and verify complex tasks, creating a scalable pipeline for generating high-quality training data [[59](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03743v1), [62](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03743)]. This can be viewed as a large-scale implementation of a multi-teacher OPD scheme, where the "teachers" are other instances of the model working in concert. Although direct citations of OPD being used to train agentic models are less prevalent in the provided sources compared to reasoning tasks, the conceptual link is strong. The challenge of applying deep reasoning patterns at deployment time, where models may over-rely on heavy reasoning even when unnecessary, is a known issue that could potentially be addressed by on-policy methods that reward efficient problem-solving [[14](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08310v2)]. The SoK paper on agentic skills identifies the training of these skills as a key open problem, and the success of OPD in other complex domains suggests it will be a critical tool in this space [[46](https://hal.science/hal-05525481v1/file/_Sok_Agent_Skills.pdf)]. The ability of OPD to bridge the gap between short-text generation and long-horizon tasks, as explored in methods like LongReD, further supports its potential applicability to the long chains of thought characteristic of agentic behavior [[77](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.524.pdf)].

## Critical Challenges and Open Problems in On-Policy Distillation

Despite its demonstrated successes, On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is not without significant challenges and empirical failure modes that have become a focal point of recent research. A primary concern is the phenomenon of **distribution shift**, where the student model's probability distribution over tokens diverges from the teacher's distribution over the course of training [[78](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25562), [130](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2601.08421)]. Since the student is constantly improving, the rollouts it generates become increasingly dissimilar to the ones the teacher was originally trained on. This can lead to the teacher providing suboptimal or misleading supervision on the student's novel mistakes, hindering further progress [[78](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25562)]. Another closely related and particularly dangerous failure mode is **policy collapse**, where the student model gets stuck in a local optimum and starts to reinforce incorrect behaviors [[49](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.865.pdf), [167](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06084v1)]. If the teacher's supervision is weak or if the student repeatedly produces poor rollouts, the loss function can guide the model away from correct solutions rather than towards them, leading to a rapid and irreversible decline in performance [[165](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04942v1), [169](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new)]. Research has shown that policy collapse is a recurring challenge in on-policy distillation, sometimes occurring when the KL divergence term in the loss becomes negligible [[165](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04942v1), [167](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06084v1)]. These issues are compounded by **teacher staleness**, especially in non-self-distillation settings, where the teacher model may not be able to keep pace with the student's rapid evolution, rendering its feedback obsolete [[128](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25562v2)].

In response to these critical challenges, a parallel stream of research has focused on developing stabilization techniques and more robust training procedures. One line of inquiry centers on **signal calibration**, which aims to modulate the supervision signal to make the training process more resilient. For example, SCOPE (Signal-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation Enhancement) proposes calibrating the distillation signal to improve the stability of the training loop [[19](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10688v1), [23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10688)]. Similarly, Entropy-Aware OPD adjusts the distillation loss based on the entropy of the teacher's logit distribution, presumably giving more weight to supervisions where the teacher is more confident [[24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07079)]. Another approach, TIP (Token Importance in On-Policy Distillation), focuses on identifying and down-weighting unimportant tokens in the rollout, which can improve efficiency and prevent noise from overwhelming the signal [[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14084v1)]. More fundamentally, some researchers are exploring ways to decouple capability from calibration, ensuring that a model's confidence in its output is well-calibrated and does not lead to overconfidence in incorrect answers [[15](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16830v1)]. Techniques like Stable On-Policy Distillation through Adaptive Target Reformulation directly tackle distribution shift by dynamically reformulating the target distribution to maintain relevance as the student evolves [[131](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07155v2)]. These methods collectively represent a crucial effort to move OPD from a fragile, empirically temperamental technique to a more robust and reliable training primitive.

Beyond stability, a major set of open problems revolves around **efficiency, scalability, and automation**. The computational cost of generating rollouts and querying a teacher model at every step can be prohibitive, especially for very large models [[125](https://openreview.net/pdf/9ea692509ee52beef37166fc2dfdec8bb759d2ef.pdf)]. Researchers are actively exploring ways to accelerate the process. Asynchronous generation, where rollout generation and model updates happen in parallel, is one such technique aimed at speeding up training [[39](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05940v1)]. Another approach is to pre-compute teacher log-probabilities over a large corpus once and then perform an offline OPD stage, though this reintroduces the risk of distribution shift if not managed carefully [[92](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13010)]. Understanding how OPD scales is another nascent but important area of research. Recent work has begun to quantify distillation scaling laws, showing that student loss follows a power law as a function of teacher quality and student size, providing a theoretical foundation for designing future experiments [[11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13016)]. A more ambitious goal is achieving true **continual learning**, where a model can learn and adapt indefinitely without catastrophic forgetting [[115](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=HlWA3V6iKF), [158](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2004506304065065334)]. OPSD is seen as a promising path toward this goal, as the self-distillation loop theoretically allows a model to remember all its past "lessons" [[158](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2004506304065065334)]. However, realizing this potential requires overcoming the aforementioned challenges of policy collapse and drift.

Finally, there are significant open problems concerning the application of OPD to new and more complex domains. The training of **agentic systems** remains a major frontier [[46](https://hal.science/hal-05525481v1/file/_Sok_Agent_Skills.pdf)]. Extending OPD principles to multi-agent collaboration, long-horizon task planning, and interaction with external environments is a non-trivial challenge that is only beginning to be explored [[59](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03743v1)]. Current methods are largely validated on single-turn or short-chain tasks, and adapting them to the persistent, dynamic nature of agentic behavior is a key area for future work [[14](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08310v2)]. Another unresolved issue is the evaluation of OPD-trained models. While benchmarks are useful, they may not capture the nuances of reliability and faithfulness in a model's reasoning process [[16](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25110v1)]. There is a need for better evaluation methodologies that can measure whether a model's generated CoT genuinely reflects its underlying reasoning or is merely a post-hoc rationalization [[16](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25110v1), [67](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05469)]. Furthermore, the impact of OPD on emergent properties of models is poorly understood [[81](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.430.pdf)]. Lastly, while the scope of this report is text-only, extending these techniques to multimodal settings presents its own unique set of challenges, such as aligning representations across different modalities [[96](https://hal.science/hal-05528300v1/file/Routing-Cascades-Survey-Feb2026.pdf)]. Addressing these open problems will be critical for unlocking the full potential of On-Policy Distillation and advancing the capabilities of future LLMs.

## Implementation-Aware Map and Foundational Resources

To synthesize the extensive landscape of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) and provide a practical guide for researchers and practitioners, this section consolidates key findings into a comprehensive map of foundational papers, industrial applications, and supporting frameworks. This map is designed to be implementation-aware, highlighting resources that are critical for anyone looking to build upon or experiment with OPD techniques. The selection of resources is based on their novelty, methodological significance, and the clarity with which they document their approaches. The following list of ten foundational papers is intended as a starting point for deep immersion into the field, covering the seminal works, the rise of self-distillation, and key methodological extensions.

### Top-10 Foundational Papers for LLM On-Policy Distillation

1.  **MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models (Agarwal et al., 2024):** This paper is frequently cited as a cornerstone of modern OPD, establishing the baseline methodology of training a student model on its self-generated rollouts with dense, token-level supervision from a teacher model, typically using a reverse KL divergence loss [[72](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08543), [82](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=IcVSKhVpKu&name=pdf), [170](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1472.pdf)]. It provides a clear and accessible introduction to the core concept.
2.  **On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Language Models (Zhao et al., 2026):** This work is a seminal paper on On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), introducing the paradigm where a single model acts as both teacher and student [[30](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10674v1), [159](https://swift.readthedocs.io/zh-cn/latest/Instruction/GKD.html)]. Its demonstration of OPSD's effectiveness in mathematical reasoning and its potential for continual learning make it a must-read [[114](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2004574638794089414), [163](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/t/2026-02-10/doc-inhmihnk1356354.shtml?froms=ggmp)].
3.  **Black-Box On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models (Anonymous, 2024):** This paper addresses the critical challenge of performing OPD when only the final output of a teacher model is accessible, proposing a generative adversarial framework to overcome this limitation [[48](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10643), [51](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10643v1)]. It is foundational for understanding the practical constraints of industrial deployment.
4.  **Breaking the Ceiling in On-Policy Distillation via Multi-Agent Debate (Anonymous, 2024):** This paper introduces a sophisticated application of Multi-Teacher OPD, using a collaborative multi-agent system to generate high-quality rollouts for distillation [[38](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01347v1)]. It is foundational for understanding the future direction of OPD towards more complex, collaborative knowledge transfer.
5.  **CRISP: Compressed Reasoning via Iterative Self-Policy Distillation (Anonymous, 2024):** This paper is a key resource for understanding how iterative self-distillation can be used not just for performance enhancement but for model compression, achieving significant reductions in reasoning trace length while improving accuracy [[41](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05433), [147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05433)].
6.  **Revisiting On-Policy Distillation: Empirical Failure Modes and ... (Anonymous, 2025):** This analysis paper is crucial for a practitioner's toolkit as it systematically investigates why OPD fails, focusing on issues like distribution shift and policy collapse [[12](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25562v1), [78](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25562)]. Understanding these failure modes is essential for building robust OPD systems.
7.  **Lightning OPD: Efficient Post-Training for Large Reasoning Models ... (Anonymous, 2024):** This work is foundational for understanding efficiency-focused enhancements to OPD, exploring methods like asynchronous generation to accelerate the training loop [[25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13010), [39](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05940v1)]. This is a critical consideration for any large-scale implementation.
8.  **Asymmetric On-Policy Distillation: Bridging Exploitation and Imitation ... (Anonymous, 2024):** This paper presents a novel methodological twist on standard OPD by introducing asymmetry in the distillation process to balance the trade-off between exploiting known good behaviors and imitating the teacher's distribution [[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06387v1)]. It represents an important evolution in loss function design.
9.  **Generalized On-Policy Distillation with Reward Extrapolation (Anonymous, 2024):** This paper contributes to the OPD+RLVR hybrid paradigm by generalizing the OPD objective using reward extrapolation, bridging the gap between pure distillation and reward-based learning [[9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12125), [42](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12125v1)].
10. **Student-in-the-Loop Chain-of-Thought Distillation via Generation ... (Anonymous, 2024):** This paper provides a detailed look at a specific application of OPD for mathematical reasoning, focusing on the iterative process of generating and refining chain-of-thought explanations [[18](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02819v1)]. It is a valuable resource for those interested in the pedagogical aspects of OPD.

### Industrial Systems and Technical Reports

The industrial adoption of OPD is most clearly documented by the technical reports and public communications from DeepSeek and GLM-5. These sources provide invaluable insights into the practical application of OPD at scale.

*   **DeepSeek (DeepSeek-V3/V4):** DeepSeek's post-training pipeline is explicitly centered around multi-teacher OPD (MOPD) to fuse specialist models into a unified generalist model [[137](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2031622077107659474), [138](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2033786457958135733)]. Their public blog posts detail the evolution of this pipeline and the increasing reliance on MOPD as a core training primitive in their latest models [[150](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/yumo-xu_deepseekv4-opd-mopd-activity-7455325567341727745-w-ey)]. They also discuss distilling from larger teachers into smaller students, a common industrial practice [[88](https://app.dahecube.com/nweb/news/20250205/225494n8bc64153d2a.htm?artid=225494)].
*   **GLM-5 (Zhipu AI):** The GLM-5 model incorporates OPD into its post-training pipeline, indicating that the technique is being adopted by other leading open-source LLM developers besides DeepSeek [[139](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404282437_Marco-MoE_Open_Multilingual_Mixture-of-Expert_Language_Models_with_Efficient_Upcycling)].
*   **Qwen (Alibaba):** While not detailing a full OPD pipeline, Qwen's documentation highlights their focus on long-context instruction tuning and post-training, areas where OPD techniques would be highly relevant [[34](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen2/), [122](https://hyper.ai/cn/papers/DeepSeek-V4)].
