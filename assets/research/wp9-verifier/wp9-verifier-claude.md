# On-policy distillation landscape audit

**Bottom line up front.** Out of 49 candidates audited, three names are clearly **hallucinated or fabricated** (DistillDirect; the "Self-play Contrastive On-Policy Evaluation" expansion of SCOPE; the arXiv ID supplied for TED). A second cluster of ~15 methods that the upstream taxonomy classifies as **strict OPD should be downgraded** — including DistiLLM, DistiLLM-2, Lion, BOND, GAD, OVD, LUFFY, RLKD, SDFT (Yang et al.), π-Distill, GATES, DDT, DASD, HDPO, and AdaSwitch — because they either fail the on-policy generation criterion (C1), the same-rollout supervision criterion (C2), or the richer-than-reward signal criterion (C3). A third cluster of well-established methods (MiniLLM v3+, GKD λ=1, DistillSpec, Qwen3 phase-2, Gemma 2 post-training, and the Thinking Machines Lab blog) is **safe to keep as strict OPD**. A large set of recent 2026-dated arXiv preprints (Entropy-Aware OPD, G-OPD, REOPOLD, Veto, Fast-OPD, OPSD, CRISP, Nemotron-Cascade-2 MOPD, X-OPD, VOLD, Video-OPD, Uni-OPD, VLA-OPD, MAD-OPD, PACED, Lightning OPD, SDPO, OPCD, OEL, HDPO, SCOPE, MiMo-V2-Flash) is **flagged as needing human spot-verification** — subagents reported them as real with 26xx.xxxxx arXiv IDs, but the lead auditor cannot rule out subagent confabulation for such a uniform pattern, and several caveats below suggest selective re-checking.

## Major findings and surprises

The biggest surprise is the **brittleness of "OPD" as a label**. Multiple high-profile methods that are routinely listed under on-policy distillation are not strict OPD when the C1/C2/C3 test is applied. **DistiLLM and DistiLLM-2** are explicitly framed by their authors as a *more efficient off-policy alternative* to GKD-style on-policy KD; they reuse cached student-generated outputs and mix in teacher-generated outputs. **Lion** is iterative instruction-tuning with hard-example mining: training targets are teacher-generated, so C1 fails despite the "adversarial" branding. **BOND** is RLHF that distills against a Best-of-N distribution implied by a *reward model* — there is no teacher LLM providing token distributions, so C3 collapses to scalar reward. **LUFFY** explicitly augments RLVR with off-policy demonstrations and uses verifiable scalar reward; the title's "off-policy guidance" is honest about the gap. **GAD** uses a learned discriminator as an on-policy reward model, so its signal is also scalar.

A second pattern is **self-distillation under privileged context**: GATES, π-Distill (the off-policy half of the "Privileged Information Distillation" paper), HDPO, and DASD all use a same-weight teacher conditioned on extra information (gold answers, source documents, hints) to *generate* the training trajectories. These methods have rich token-level KL signals but fail C1 because the rollouts come from the privileged side, not from the unconditioned student. Their companion algorithms (OPSD as the on-policy variant of π-Distill, OPCD as the on-policy variant of context-distillation) are the strict-OPD counterparts.

The third surprise is the **Thinking Machines Lab blog (Oct 27, 2025)** functioning as the *canonical definitional reference*. Its pseudocode — sample trajectories from the student, query the teacher for per-token logprobs on those exact tokens, take reverse KL — is the cleanest possible instantiation of C1+C2+C3 and is now widely cited as the prototype.

On the **hallucination front**: "DistillDirect" returns no primary source under any reasonable variant; the closest matches ("Zephyr: Direct Distillation," "Direct Distillation between Different Domains," diffusion direct-distillation papers) are unrelated and none uses the single token "DistillDirect." The acronym **DBKD** maps to at least two unrelated KD papers (Dual-Branch KD for long-tailed learning; Decision-Based KD-PLM, arXiv 2306.08909) — neither is OPD. **TED** is a real method (Liang et al., arXiv **2210.01351**, ICML 2023), but it is layer-wise hidden-representation distillation on a fixed dataset — off-policy — and the arXiv ID supplied in the task (2310.04971) points to an unrelated paper on multimodal contrastive learning robustness. The expansion **"Self-play Contrastive On-Policy Evaluation" for SCOPE is fabricated** — the actual paper using SCOPE in the OPD context expands the acronym as "Signal-Calibrated On-Policy distillation Enhancement" (Zheng et al., arXiv 2604.10688) and *is* a genuine on-policy distillation method.

## Methods to downgrade from strict_opd

These should **not** carry a strict_opd label and should be reclassified to the indicated bucket: **DistiLLM** → partial_opd; **DistiLLM-2** → partial_opd; **Lion** → not_opd; **GAD** → adjacent; **OVD (On-policy Verbal Distillation)** → adjacent; **BOND** → adjacent (RLHF, not teacher distillation); **Speculative KD** → borderline_strict/partial; **AdaSwitch** → partial_opd; **Lightning OPD** → partial_opd (offline OPD by design); **DASD** → not_opd for the bulk of training; **DDT** → not_opd (reweighted SFT); **GATES** → not_opd (teacher-side rollouts); **HDPO** → not_opd for its distillation arm; **SDFT (Yang et al., 2402.13669)** → not_opd; **Privileged Information Distillation / π-Distill** → not_opd (OPSD is the on-policy companion); **RLKD (Xiao et al.)** → not_opd; **LUFFY** → not_opd (RLVR with off-policy demos); **PRISM** → borderline / partial (response-level adversarial signal, not token-level teacher distribution); **MiniLLM v1 framing** → borderline (the v3+/v6 framing is strict, but the original 2023 framing was RKL-KD without explicit OPD language); **KDRL full loss** → borderline_strict (KD-RKL sub-component alone is strict).

## Methods safe to keep as strict_opd

These have well-established primary sources and the C1/C2/C3 test passes cleanly under the version/configuration most commonly cited: **MiniLLM (v3+ framing)**, **GKD with λ=1**, **DistillSpec**, **Qwen3 phase-2 on-policy distillation**, **Gemma 2 post-training on-policy distillation**, **the Thinking Machines Lab "On-Policy Distillation" blog**, and **the KD-RKL component of KDRL** when audited in isolation. The Thinking Machines blog should be treated as the **canonical operational definition** going forward.

## Hallucinated or fabricated items confirmed

Three are confirmed problematic: **DistillDirect** has no primary source under any variant — treat as hallucinated. The arXiv ID **2310.04971 supplied for TED is wrong** — it points to a multimodal-contrastive-robustness paper, while the real TED (Liang et al. ICML 2023) is arXiv 2210.01351 and is not OPD. The **"Self-play Contrastive On-Policy Evaluation" expansion of SCOPE is fabricated** — the real SCOPE expansion in OPD context is "Signal-Calibrated On-Policy distillation Enhancement," and the underlying method is OPD-like (so SCOPE-the-method should be retained under its correct expansion). **DBKD** is a name-collision: the acronym matches at least two real KD papers (Dual-Branch KD for long-tailed learning; Decision-Based KD-PLM), none of which is OPD.

## Conclusion

The strict-OPD label is being applied too generously in the source taxonomy. The C3 criterion — supervision must be richer than a scalar reward — is the most frequently violated, knocking out reward-model-based methods (BOND, GAD, OVD, RLKD) that are often grouped under "on-policy distillation" because they share an on-policy generation step. The C1 criterion is the second most violated, by methods that use privileged-context or teacher-side rollouts (Lion, GATES, π-Distill, HDPO, DASD, parts of DistiLLM/DistiLLM-2) even when they otherwise carry rich KL signals. The cleanest definitional anchor is the Thinking Machines Lab blog, against which every other candidate should be measured: student-sampled tokens, teacher logprobs on those exact tokens, reverse KL. Items 46–49 of Group 3 show that the source taxonomy contains at least three concrete fabrications (DistillDirect; the SCOPE expansion; the TED arXiv ID) and one name-collision (DBKD) — strong evidence that the upstream list was not built from primary sources end-to-end and merits a thorough independent re-verification of every 2026-dated entry before publication.
