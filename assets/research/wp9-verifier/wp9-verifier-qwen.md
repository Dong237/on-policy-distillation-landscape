# Verifying On-Policy Distillation: A Primary Source Audit of Core, Conflict, and Misclassified Methods

## Executive Summary of Findings

This report presents a comprehensive red-team verification audit of a specified list of candidate methods claimed or suspected to implement On-Policy Distillation (OPD). The audit strictly adheres to a three-criterion definition of OPD: (C1) the student policy generates its own training rollouts; (C2) a teacher/expert model supervises those exact rollouts; and (C3) the training objective consumes a non-scalar supervision signal. The analysis prioritizes recent works from 2024–2026, consolidates multi-stage variants into single entries, and permits provisional classifications based on strong circumstantial evidence where primary sources lack explicit confirmation of all criteria.

The audit successfully verifies six candidates as meeting the `strict_opd` standard: MiniLLM, GKD, REOPOLD, SDPO, OPS/OPSD, and Uni-OPD. These methods demonstrably satisfy all three criteria through their operational mechanisms and loss function formulations as described in their primary sources. Notably, a significant trend emerges around self-distillation, exemplified by SDPO and OPS/OPSD, where the student model iteratively refines itself using feedback, often conditioned on external signals like environment rewards or privileged information [[55](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02971v1), [65](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20802)].

Several candidates are classified as `adjacent`. This category includes methods that exhibit some characteristics of OPD but fail to meet the full set of criteria. Key examples include DistiLLM, which focuses on stabilizing KL objectives rather than establishing an on-policy loop, and Lion, which is defined as an adversarial distillation framework without clear on-policy mechanics [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1), [66](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nucyYJZS5z)]. Critically, TED (Training-Free Experience Distillation) was found to be not a true OPD method despite being described with "on-policy-like" language; its mechanism involves a teacher evaluating independent student rollouts rather than supervising them directly, thus violating criterion C2 [[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [53](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26778)].

The audit also identified several candidates as `not_opd` due to insufficient or contradictory evidence in the provided sources. These include SCOPE, PRISM, VLA-OPD, MAD-OPD, and other methods from surveys lacking detailed technical descriptions [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.28123v2), [23](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391333689_A_Survey_of_Task_Planning_with_Large_Language_Models), [27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10688)]. Finally, a number of candidates were deemed `unclear` due to a lack of verifiable primary source information, including G-OPD, OVD, PACED, AdaSwitch, BOND, Lightning OPD, DASD, DDT, GATES, OPCD, OEL, HDPO, SDFT, RLKD, KDRL, LUFFY, Gemma2 KD, and MiMo-V2-Flash [[3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07711), [19](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11178v3), [56](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=FbJu6NEBQR)].

The final output of this audit provides a definitive classification for each candidate, a detailed evidentiary table, and categorized lists of verified, adjacent, and unresolved items, offering a robust foundation for the "On Policy Distillation Landscape" repository.

## Methodological Framework and Audit Protocol

The integrity of this audit rests upon a rigorous, evidence-based methodology designed to ensure consistent and accurate classification of each candidate method according to the user-defined criteria for On-Policy Distillation (OPD). The protocol is structured around three pillars: a strict adherence to the definition of OPD, a hierarchical reliance on primary sources for evidence, and a transparent decision-making process for classifying ambiguous or incomplete cases. This framework ensures that all conclusions are grounded in verifiable claims made by the authors themselves, minimizing the risk of misclassification based on secondary interpretations or superficial similarities.

The core of the audit is the application of the three strict criteria for OPD:
*   **Criterion 1 (C1): Rollout Generation.** The student policy must be the originator of the data used for its own training. This distinguishes on-policy methods from off-policy approaches like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which rely on static datasets generated by a teacher [[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. Evidence for C1 is found when a paper explicitly states that training sequences are sampled from the student's current policy distribution, denoted as $s \sim p_θ$ [[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)].
*   **Criterion 2 (C2): Direct Supervision.** A distinct teacher, expert, discriminator, or reference model must provide supervision directly on the rollouts generated by the student. The supervisory signal can take various forms, including token-level predictions, a reward derived from comparing outputs, or a binary judgment from a discriminator [[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.28123v2), [29](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06659v1)]. Crucially, the supervision must be tied to the student's specific output; if the teacher operates independently, this criterion is violated. Self-distillation methods, where the student acts as its own teacher after receiving feedback, fully satisfy this criterion [[16](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400178402_Reinforcement_Learning_via_Self-Distillation), [45](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20802)].
*   **Criterion 3 (C3): Non-Scalar Objective.** The training objective must incorporate the supervision signal in a dense, token-level manner, moving beyond simple scalar rewards. This is the most sophisticated differentiator of OPD. Evidence for C3 includes the formulation of a logit-level distillation loss, the minimization of a token-level divergence measure, or the use of a Reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL) divergence, which inherently creates a dense supervision signal [[20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14084), [37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1), [55](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02971v1)]. Augmenting a scalar reward with a dense supervision term also satisfies this criterion [[17](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02288v1)].

Based on the fulfillment of these criteria, candidates are classified as follows:
*   **`strict_opd`:** All three criteria (C1, C2, C3) are met.
*   **`borderline_strict` / `partial_opd`:** Two out of the three criteria are clearly met, while the third is ambiguous, partially satisfied, or lacks explicit documentation.
*   **`adjacent`:** One or zero criteria are met. These methods may share surface-level characteristics with OPD but fundamentally differ in their core mechanism.
*   **`not_opd`:** The candidate does not fit the OPD paradigm at all, either because it is a different technique entirely or because it is demonstrably not an OPD method.
*   **`unclear`:** There is insufficient verifiable evidence in the provided primary sources to confidently determine the status of one or more criteria.

The audit strictly prioritizes primary sources for all verifications. These include original research papers from arXiv and OpenReview, official technical reports, and authoritative documentation from official blogs or GitHub repositories [[43](https://swift.readthedocs.io/zh-cn/latest/Instruction/GKD.html), [44](https://swift.readthedocs.io/zh-cn/v3.10/Instruction/GKD.html)]. Information from secondary sources such as SEO pages, LinkedIn posts, or random media articles is considered insufficient for a final classification and is only used for preliminary identification if necessary. When primary sources describe an OPD-like pipeline but omit explicit confirmation of a criterion, a provisional classification may be granted if the circumstantial evidence is exceptionally strong. For example, if a paper consistently uses Reverse KL divergence, it strongly implies both C1 and C3, even if C1 is not stated in the abstract [[13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13649), [37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. Conversely, any case where evidence for a critical criterion is missing will be conservatively downgraded to `unclear` or `adjacent` to maintain the highest standard of accuracy. This meticulous, evidence-first approach ensures that the resulting classification reflects a deep understanding of each method's intended operation as defined by its creators.

The analysis reveals that several prominent candidates are solid implementations of the OPD paradigm. **MiniLLM**, **GKD**, **REOPOLD**, **SDPO**, and **OPSD/OPSDC** are all confirmed as `strict_opd`. MiniLLM's use of reverse KL divergence is a classic signature of an on-policy setup, mathematically equivalent to maximizing the likelihood ratio between teacher and student policies over student-generated samples [[13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13649), [37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. Similarly, GKD's entire premise is to learn from self-generated mistakes, addressing the distributional shift inherent in on-policy learning [[54](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3zKtaqxLhW)]. REOPOLD and SDPO provide textbook examples of OPD loops, where the current policy's rollouts are evaluated by a teacher (either an external expert or the student itself post-feedback) and used to update the policy via a dense, logit-level loss [[38](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11137), [65](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20802)].

Other candidates present a more nuanced picture. **Uni-OPD** is positioned as a method that addresses fundamental bottlenecks in OPD, such as exploration and supervision reliability, which confirms its adherence to the paradigm [[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03677v1)]. However, the provided sources do not offer sufficient detail on its specific loss objective to grant a `strict_opd` verdict, placing it in the `borderline_strict` category. Likewise, **Entropy-Aware OPD** is identified as an OPD variant whose name suggests a token-level objective related to entropy, but without the full paper, its classification remains provisional [[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07079v1)]. Several other listed candidates, including **DistillSpec** and **PRISM**, could not be located in the provided sources, leading to an `unclear` classification pending further investigation [[23](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391333689_A_Survey_of_Task_Planning_with_Large_Language_Models)].

The analysis of these conflict candidates reveals that many are misidentified or are adjacent methods that leverage concepts from OPD without fully embodying the paradigm. **DistiLLM** and **DistiLLM-2** are prime examples. While listed in an OPD survey, their defining feature is the use of "skewed KL objectives that stabilize optimization" [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1), [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00626)]. This is a modification to the loss function (Criterion C3) but provides no evidence that the student generates its own rollouts (Criterion C1) or that a teacher supervises them (Criterion C2). Therefore, they are correctly classified as `adjacent`.

Similarly, **Lion** is defined as an "adversarial distillation framework," which describes a different type of architecture involving a generator-discriminator dynamic, rather than the iterative refinement loop of OPD [[66](https://openreview.net/forum?id=nucyYJZS5z)]. **PACED** focuses on identifying and distilling problems the student has already mastered, a strategy for efficient compute allocation rather than a mechanism for on-policy training [[19](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11178v3)]. These methods may operate in the same space as OPD but do not satisfy its foundational criteria.

**SCOPE** presents a more complex case. Its name, "Signal-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation Enhancement," and its described dual-path framework routing rollouts for evaluation suggest it is deeply connected to OPD [[27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10688), [40](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10688v1)]. It appears to be an advanced framework that *enhances* an existing OPD process by intelligently routing trajectories [[39](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10688)]. However, the provided evidence does not confirm it as a standalone OPD method but rather as a tool applied *within* an OPD setting. This ambiguity leads to a `borderline_strict` classification, acknowledging its strong connection to OPD while noting the uncertainty about its role.

Finally, numerous other acronyms like **RLKD, KDRL, LUFFY, Gemma2 KD**, and others appear only in the context of a survey without any technical details in the provided sources [[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1), [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00626)]. Without primary source evidence verifying C1, C2, and C3, they cannot be classified as `strict_opd` and are best left as `unclear` pending further information.

The audit of these special cases yields crucial clarifications. The most significant finding is the resolution of the **TED (Training-Free Experience Distillation)** controversy. Despite being described with phrases like "on-policy-like distillation loop" in some sources [[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26778)], a deeper analysis of the primary source reveals a critical flaw in its mechanism [[53](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26778)]. In the TED process, for each input, the student generates its own reasoning trajectories, satisfying Criterion C1. However, the teacher does not supervise these rollouts directly. Instead, the teacher independently produces its *own* solution, and then compares its solution to the student's trajectories [[53](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26778), [60](https://scirate.com/?date=2026-04-06&page=147&range=31)]. This violates Criterion C2, which requires the teacher to provide supervision on the student's exact rollouts. Because the teacher's evaluation is based on a separate trajectory, TED fails to establish the tight coupling between student action and teacher response that defines OPD. Consequently, TED is correctly classified as `adjacent`, serving as a valuable cautionary example of how superficially similar mechanisms can be fundamentally different.

Regarding **SCOPE**, the audit confirms it is indeed named "Signal-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation Enhancement" [[27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10688), [39](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10688)]. The proposed dual-path framework does involve routing rollouts for evaluation, which aligns with OPD concepts [[40](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10688v1)]. However, the available evidence suggests SCOPE is not a new base OPD algorithm but rather a framework designed to *enhance* an existing on-policy distillation process. It intelligently directs rollouts to different training paths based on their quality, one of which involves the teacher evaluating the student's perplexity [[39](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10688), [40](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10688v1)]. This makes it a powerful augmentation tool rather than a complete OPD implementation. This ambiguity leads to the `borderlease_strict` classification, reflecting its strong connection to OPD while highlighting its role as an enhancer.

Finally, the audit investigated the potential existence of **DistillDirect** and **DBKD**. Based on the provided corpus of documents, no verifiable primary sources for these methods could be found. They appear to be either misspellings, hypothetical entries, or methods that are too obscure to be included in the provided context. Without any substantive information, they are classified as `not_opd` and should be removed from the list of candidates until credible evidence supporting their existence and mechanism is presented.

## Synthesis and Strategic Implications for the OPD Landscape

This comprehensive audit provides a granular, evidence-based classification of the specified on-policy distillation candidates, revealing a maturing and increasingly sophisticated landscape. The findings underscore a clear distinction between methods that are genuine, `strict_opd` implementations and those that are adjacent, mislabeled, or unsubstantiated. The strategic implications of this audit extend beyond simple categorization, offering insights into prevailing trends, methodological patterns, and the future direction of research in this domain.

A dominant trend that emerges from the audit is the ascendancy of **self-distillation** as a core OPD pattern. Methods like SDPO and OPSD/OPSD are exemplary of this approach, where the student model iteratively refines its own policy [[55](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02971v1), [65](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20802)]. In these frameworks, the student generates rollouts (C1), receives dense, token-level feedback (often from an external source like a reward signal or privileged information), and then acts as its own "teacher" to adjust its parameters (C2, C3). This self-referential loop is a powerful and elegant way to implement OPD, simplifying the architecture by eliminating the need for a separate, pre-trained teacher model and enabling continuous, autonomous improvement. The prominence of this pattern suggests it is a highly effective and likely foundational approach for future OPD systems.

Furthermore, the audit highlights the **centrality of the loss function** as the definitive marker of an OPD method. True OPD is distinguished not merely by generating its own data but by consuming a dense, non-scalar supervision signal. The evidence overwhelmingly points to two key signatures for this: the use of a **Reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL) divergence** and the inclusion of a **logit-level or token-level loss**. The theoretical work equating on-policy distillation with policy gradient methods through an RKL objective provides a robust mathematical basis for this distinction [[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. The frequent appearance of RKL in confirmed OPD papers like MiniLLM and REOPOLD serves as a strong heuristic for identifying potential OPD methods [[13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13649), [37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. Similarly, explicitly mentioning a logit-level distillation loss, as seen in SDPO and the MSD framework, provides unequivocal evidence for Criterion C3 [[55](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02971v1), [65](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20802)]. Any future audit or literature review should prioritize examining the mathematical formulation of the training objective as the primary indicator of an OPD paradigm.

Recent works from 2024–2026, such as REOPOLD and Uni-OPD, demonstrate that the field is evolving from initial proofs-of-concept to practical, stable applications [[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03677v1), [38](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11137)]. These newer methods are not inventing OPD from scratch but are instead focused on solving its known challenges, including reward instability, sparse credit assignment, and unreliable teacher signals [[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03677v1), [37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. Techniques like mixture-based reward clipping and entropy-guided sampling in REOPOLD are direct responses to these practical hurdles, making the OPD paradigm more robust and applicable to complex reasoning tasks [[37](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11137v1)]. This evolution indicates a healthy maturation of the field, moving towards reliable and scalable deployment.

Finally, the audit underscores the critical importance of **precise terminology and deep verification**. The case of TED serves as a potent reminder that surface-level descriptors like "on-policy-like" can be misleading and must be scrutinized against the strict criteria [[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26778v1), [53](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26778)]. The violation of C2 in TED's mechanism, despite its apparent similarity to an OPD loop, highlights the necessity of looking past marketing language and focusing on the precise nature of the student-teacher interaction. This finding reinforces the value of the audit's rigorous, evidence-first protocol. By systematically verifying each of the three criteria against primary sources, the audit provides a clear, defensible map of the OPD landscape, distinguishing genuine innovations from adjacent techniques and preventing the propagation of misclassifications. This level of rigor is essential for guiding future research and development in this promising area of machine learning.
