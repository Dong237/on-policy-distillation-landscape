# On-Policy Distillation for Text-Only LLMs

As of **May 11, 2026**, the text-only LLM OPD literature has converged around a clear core idea: let the **student generate its own trajectories**, then attach **dense supervision** to those trajectories from a teacher distribution, a verifier-conditioned self-teacher, a discriminator, or another privileged signal. What changed in 2026 is not just scale; it is the recognition that OPD is best understood as a family of **interactive post-training algorithms** sitting between knowledge distillation and reinforcement learning, with major subfamilies for white-box logit matching, self-distillation under privileged context, black-box adversarial distillation, and OPD–RL hybrids. The strongest public progress is concentrated in **math reasoning, code reasoning, long-CoT stabilization, and agentic/tool-use settings**, while industrial reports now use OPD-like stages to recover capabilities lost in long multi-stage post-training pipelines. citeturn11search2turn38view3turn8search3turn37view0turn35view2

## Criteria and taxonomy

I use your definition as the primary filter. In this report, an item is **Strict OPD** only when the student or policy generates its own trajectories during training and some teacher, verifier, reference model, discriminator, or privileged-context variant supplies supervision on those same student-generated trajectories. I mark methods **Partial OPD** when they relax one of those requirements, most often by using **frozen offline rollouts**, **stale near-policy rollouts**, or a substantial **off-policy tutor trajectory** component. I mark items **Adjacent** when they are mainly diagnostic, analytical, or system reports rather than standalone OPD algorithms. citeturn11search2turn21search0turn28view5turn29view3

The most useful taxonomy for “On Policy Distillation Landscape” has five orthogonal axes. The survey literature already organizes OPD by **feedback signal**, **teacher access**, and **loss granularity**; for implementation work, two more axes matter just as much: **rollout freshness** and **training stage**. In practice, the field now clusters into: **white-box token-level OPD**; **self-distillation with privileged context**; **black-box OPD**; **OPD interpreted as dense KL-regularized RL**; and **agentic long-horizon OPD**, where prefix drift and error accumulation dominate the failure landscape. citeturn11search2turn15search6turn38view3turn10view11turn28view5

This also gives the cleanest line between OPD and nearby methods. **Ordinary SFT and offline KD** are not OPD because the student does not learn on its own trajectories. **Pure RLHF and RLVR** are not OPD when supervision is only a scalar reward with no trajectory-level distillation target, although several 2026 papers explicitly show how OPD can be written as dense KL-constrained RL or fused with verifier feedback. **Online preference methods** are adjacent unless they also provide teacher-side token supervision on student rollouts. citeturn30view9turn38view3turn32view1turn24search2

## Historical timeline

The field starts in earnest with **urlMiniLLMturn9view0** in mid-2023, which made reverse-KL, student-generated rollouts, and large-LLM instruction distillation concrete for decoder-only models, and with **urlOn-Policy Distillation of Language Modelsturn5search4** from entity["event","ICLR 2024","International Conference on Learning Representations 2024"], which formalized Generalized Knowledge Distillation and showed OPD on summarization, translation, GSM8K-style reasoning, and instruction tuning, including a clean bridge to RL fine-tuning. citeturn30view0turn30view4turn30view5turn30view8turn30view9

By 2025, OPD moved from an academic curiosity into public post-training practice. The **urlQwen3 Technical Reportturn4search0** documents a strong-to-weak pipeline with an explicit **off-policy stage followed by an on-policy stage** for lightweight models, and reports much lower cost than running full multi-stage RL separately for every smaller model. The **urlThinking Machines Lab OPD blogturn13search0** and **urlPractical On-Policy Distillation for Continual Learningturn13search1** made the engineering case that OPD had become a serious post-training primitive rather than a one-off algorithm. At the same time, **urlBlack-Box On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Modelsturn38view0** showed that even proprietary teachers can be distilled on-policy via an adversarial discriminator instead of teacher logits. citeturn37view0turn13search0turn13search1turn38view0

In early 2026, the literature exploded. January brought **Veto**, **OPSD**, **SDFT**, and **SDPO**, which broadened OPD from teacher–student logit matching into adaptive target reformulation, privileged self-teaching from demonstrations, and verifier-feedback self-distillation. February reframed OPD as **dense KL-constrained RL** through **G-OPD / ExOPD** and extended it into **context distillation** with **OPCD**. March and April then split into three branches: **theory and mechanics** such as **REOPOLD**, **PACED**, **Rethinking OPD**, and **Revisiting OPD**; **efficiency and stability** such as **Entropy-Aware OPD**, **TIP**, **Lightning OPD**, **CaOPD**, and later **vOPD**; and **agentic/long-horizon OPD** such as **TCOD**, **Skill-SD**, **Prune-OPD**, and newer watchlist papers like **SOD**, **Near-Policy**, and **MAD-OPD**. citeturn38view1turn32view0turn29view0turn24search3turn22search7turn38view3turn8search3turn38view2turn11search11turn10view11turn10view8turn10view12turn21search0turn29view9turn28view5turn28view4turn28view6turn21search1turn20search1turn20search13turn17search0

## Open problems and repository structure

The most important unresolved problem is **teacher reliability on drifted prefixes**. This is the root cause behind several seemingly different 2026 papers: **TCOD** uses trajectory-depth curricula, **TIP** selects high-value token regions, **Prune-OPD** cuts off drifted long-horizon reasoning, and **Skill-SD** injects dynamic skill summaries so the teacher signal remains locally meaningful. The fact that four different design lines are all trying to repair “teacher becomes untrustworthy once the student wanders off” is a strong sign that this is the central research bottleneck for long-CoT and agentic OPD. citeturn28view5turn10view12turn28view6turn28view4

The second open problem is **when OPD should be treated as RL and when it should not**. **G-OPD**, **REOPOLD**, and **vOPD** all point toward an RL interpretation, while **Veto**, **TIP**, and **PACED** suggest a distillation-native view where target shaping, token choice, and curriculum matter more than a plain policy-gradient reading. I expect the next wave of work to standardize this split: RL-style variance reduction and clipping at the systems level, paired with distillation-specific routing and calibration at the signal-design level. citeturn38view3turn8search3turn28view8turn38view1turn10view12turn38view2

A third unresolved issue is **teacher–student compatibility and novelty**. **Rethinking OPD** argues that the teacher must offer genuinely new capability and a compatible thinking pattern; otherwise the student sees little useful gradient or even degrades. This directly explains why some same-family weak-to-strong setups disappoint, why cross-family tokenizer mismatches are painful, and why industrial reports increasingly use **intermediate teachers from the same pipeline** instead of arbitrary external teachers. citeturn11search11turn17search8turn10view11turn35view2

A fourth problem is **confidence and calibration**. **CaOPD** shows that privileged teacher context can improve task performance while systematically breaking confidence estimation, which matters a lot for product deployments and tool-using agents. This is likely to become more important as OPD moves from competition math into agentic systems that need calibrated stopping, fallback, or escalation policies. citeturn29view9turn32view5

A fifth challenge is **strictness versus practicality**. The engineering pressure to reduce teacher-serving cost is pushing the field toward **offline OPD** and **near-policy async OPD**; **Lightning OPD** demonstrates the upside, but it also shows exactly how the strict online formulation gets weakened. In repository terms, this means OPD papers should always be tagged not just by teacher access and signal type, but also by **rollout freshness**: strict on-policy, near-policy, or frozen-rollout. citeturn21search0turn32view4turn20search1

For **On Policy Distillation Landscape**, the cleanest top-level structure is:

- **Foundations**
- **White-box token-level OPD**
- **Self-distillation and privileged context**
- **Black-box OPD**
- **OPD + RL / RLVR hybrids**
- **Agentic and long-horizon OPD**
- **Industrial reports**
- **Frameworks and trainers**
- **Diagnostics, failure modes, and calibration**
- **Benchmarks and evaluation caveats**

I would also add four tags to every row in the repository: **strictness**, **teacher access**, **rollout freshness**, and **domain**. That tagging scheme is what most existing OPD lists still lack, and it is also the shortest path from a taxonomy-first survey to an implementation-aware research map. citeturn11search2turn15search6turn21search0turn28view5
