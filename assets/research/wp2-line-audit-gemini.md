Boundary calls follow the WP2 decision rules: (i) response-level discriminator/adversarial feedback is `borderline_strict` at best unless C3 clearly consumes non-scalar teacher-style supervision on exact student rollouts; (ii) verbal/API/judge scores are usually `partial_opd` or `adjacent` unless converted into a distillation objective; (iii) DPO/ORPO are not strict unless online same-rollout teacher supervision + a distillation-style objective beyond static preference fitting are both proven; (iv) RLVR/PPO/GRPO with reward-only signal is not OPD; (v) teacher-generated SFT traces are offline KD/SFT.

## Memo

Of the 5 WP2 candidates audited, **0** pass as `strict_opd`, **1** is `borderline_strict` (PRISM), **3** downgrade to `partial_opd` (GAD, OVD, ORPO-Distill), and **1** downgrades to `adjacent` (SODA — explicitly *semi*-on-policy with a frozen, one-time student snapshot). No name collisions were detected inside this queue, so no new papers were added.

**Borderline strict — PRISM.** [arXiv:2604.28123v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28123) explicitly satisfies C1 ("response-level adversarial game between the policy and a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) discriminator") and gives a *non-scalar* corrective signal ("disentangled corrective signals … perception and reasoning experts"), not just one reward scalar. C2 is satisfied by a discriminator trained against Gemini 3 Flash demonstrations. Crucially, however, supervision is response-level adversarial probability, not a teacher distribution on student tokens, and PRISM is positioned as an *intermediate* alignment stage before downstream RLVR. I keep `borderline_strict`: stronger than reward-only PG, weaker than per-token KL OPD.

**Partial OPD downgrades:**
- **GAD** ([arXiv:2511.10643v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10643)) — C1 satisfied ("frames the student LLM as a generator … minimax game"), but C3 explicitly collapses the discriminator into a *scalar* on-policy reward: "The discriminator acts as an on-policy reward model that co-evolves with the student, providing stable, adaptive feedback." The objective is response-level GAN/PG with a learned scalar; per the WP2 response-level-discriminator rule, this is `partial_opd`, not `borderline_strict` (no non-scalar teacher-style supervision is asserted in the abstract or §1).
- **OVD** ([arXiv:2601.21968v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21968)) — C1 satisfied ("allowing the student model to freely explore the output space"), but supervision is an ordinal scalar 0–9 verbal score per trajectory: "memory-efficient framework that replaces token-level probability matching with trajectory matching using discrete verbal scores (0–9) from teacher models." Per the WP2 verbal-score rule, scalar verbal score → `partial_opd` (RLVR-flavored).
- **ORPO-Distill** ([arXiv:2509.25100v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25100)) — explicitly *mixed-policy*, not on-policy: "adopts a mixed-policy strategy for utilizing student-generated outputs, outperforming both off- and on-policy alternatives." C2 is teacher text traces (no logits), and C3 is ORPO contrastive preference, not a same-rollout teacher distribution. Static preference optimization → `partial_opd` per the DPO/ORPO rule.

**Adjacent (downgrade beyond partial):**
- **SODA** ([arXiv:2604.03873v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03873)) — author-self-named "Semi On-Policy Black-Box Distillation". Student rollouts are explicitly frozen: "a one-time static snapshot of the student's responses." This violates C1 (no current-policy rollout per update). The objective is contrastive pairing of teacher responses against the *static* student snapshot, "eliminating the need for costly dynamic rollouts and fragile adversarial training." Static snapshot + contrastive loss → fails C1 + C3 → `adjacent`.

**No new candidates added.** All 5 acronyms map to a single primary source; no collisions inside the queue.

**Caveats:**
- 26xx.* identifiers are post-knowledge-cutoff preprints; I rely on arXiv abstracts + first-page introductions (HTML/PDF) accessed directly. C1/C2/C3 quotes are taken from those locations.
- For GAD I do not have a §-level breakdown of whether a non-scalar feature distillation accompanies the scalar reward; abstract clearly says "reward model", so I keep `partial_opd`.
- For PRISM, the "disentangled" corrective signal is non-scalar in spirit (multi-expert), but evaluation remains response-level — so the upgrade stops at `borderline_strict`.
