### Method Families Found

1. Adversarial black-box OPD-like loops: GAD and PRISM. These are the strongest black-box candidates because the current policy produces rollouts and an adaptive discriminator supervises those rollouts against teacher or supervision-pool responses.
2. Verbal-score OPD-like loops: OVD. The student samples trajectories, teacher or environment agents assign 0-9 verbal scores, low-scoring trajectories are rejected or replaced, and policy optimization uses the resulting rewards.
3. Preference or contrastive black-box distillation: ORPO-Distill and PAD. These use teacher/student traces or teacher preference distributions, but the objective is ORPO/DPO/preference matching over response sets, usually not a same-rollout distillation loss.
4. API curriculum and teacher-generated SFT: Lion, SuperCorrect stage 1, Distill step-by-step, MAGDI/QCRD-style rationale distillation, DeepSeek-R1 distilled checkpoints. These are important false positives because they use black-box teachers but train on teacher-generated traces or static preference/correction datasets.
5. Reward-only RLVR and GRPO neighbors: R1-style RLVR, VLM-R1, reward-model-only GRPO, rejection sampling. These satisfy on-policy rollout in some cases, but they do not satisfy C2/C3 unless a teacher-style signal is consumed as distillation supervision on the same rollout.

### What Is Actually Black-Box OPD Versus Adjacent

No audited WP2 method should be promoted to `strict_opd` under the current repository definition without further line-level/code audit. GAD and PRISM are legitimate black-box on-policy distillation-adjacent methods and should remain `partial_opd` or `borderline_strict`: C1 and C2 are strong, but C3 is consumed through discriminator reward, GRPO, or policy-gradient advantages rather than token/logit or clearly non-scalar distillation loss. OVD is `partial_opd`: it explicitly replaces token probability matching with verbal trajectory matching, but its objective is GRPO/PPO-style reward optimization over accepted student and teacher trajectories.

ORPO-Distill is `partial_opd` at most. It uses student-generated negative traces and an on-policy or mixed-policy update option, but teacher supervision is mostly pair construction against teacher positives, not teacher evaluation of the exact student rollout. Lion, SuperCorrect, PAD, DeepSeek-R1 distilled checkpoints, static DPO/ORPO/SimPO, SeqKD, rejection sampling, and reward-only GRPO/RLVR are adjacent or not OPD.

### Key Differences From White-Box OPD

White-box OPD has dense teacher distributions or log-probs on the exact current student tokens, so C2 and C3 are usually auditable through KL, reverse-KL, generalized-KL, or token-level teacher-logprob objectives. Black-box/API OPD cannot access that distribution. It replaces teacher logits with samples, ratings, rankings, verbal scores, discriminators, or API-generated critiques. That makes it more cross-architecture and cross-tokenizer friendly, but much weaker for strict OPD verification because the signal is often sequence-level and scalar.

The practical boundary is C3: adversarial distribution alignment and verbal rejection sampling are distillation-flavored, but if the policy only maximizes a scalar reward or static preference objective, the method is not strict OPD under this repository's rules.

### Practical Costs And Failure Modes

- API cost: Lion reports roughly 450k `gpt-3.5-turbo` calls and about 900 USD for one student. GAD collects 200K GPT-5-Chat teacher responses before adversarial training. PRISM curates Gemini-derived supervision and trains an MoE discriminator. OVD reduces logit memory but still needs teacher/environment scoring over many trajectories or steps.
- Response inflation and reward hacking: GAD explicitly reports that an off-policy discriminator led to reward hacking and very long responses, while the on-policy co-trained discriminator stayed stable.
- Discriminator overfitting and saturation: PRISM warm-starts policy and discriminator because a large initial policy-supervision gap can saturate the discriminator. GAD mitigates stale reward by updating the discriminator online.
- Teacher drift and API versioning: API teachers and judges can change over time. Prompt templates, decoding parameters, and hidden safety filters become part of the method and may not be reproducible.
- Calibration and prompt sensitivity: Lion uses two referee orders to reduce positional bias. OVD relies on discrete score thresholds and teacher score calibration. PAD depends on teacher-as-reward preference calibration.
- Response-level credit assignment: sequence scores and discriminator rewards do not identify which token or step should change unless the method supplies structured step-level feedback and a loss that consumes it directly.
- Mixed trajectories: OVD replaces rejected student trajectories with teacher demonstrations; ORPO-Distill mixes base and current student negatives; SuperCorrect and Lion use static or teacher-generated targets. These weaken C1 even when a student output appears in the pipeline.

## Bottom Line

WP2 should not add any new `strict_opd` black-box/API LLM method today. The safest updates are:

- Keep PRISM as `borderline_strict`, scoped to its adversarial alignment stage.
- Keep GAD and OVD as `partial_opd`.
- Add ORPO-Distill only as `partial_opd` if the main table intentionally includes mixed-policy preference-distillation neighbors.
- Keep Lion, SuperCorrect, PAD, static preference optimization, teacher-generated SFT traces, rejection sampling, and reward-only GRPO/RLVR in adjacent or false-positive ledgers unless a future line audit finds same-rollout distillation supervision consumed by the objective.
